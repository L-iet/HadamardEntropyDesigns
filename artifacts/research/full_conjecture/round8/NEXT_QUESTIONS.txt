NEXT CONSTRUCTION QUESTIONS

1. Use models rich enough for the residue class.
The sharp cubic H12/H20 encodings are benchmarks with actual polynomial
manifests. Full minimal-seed encodings of large N=4mod8 need degree at
leastceil(r/2). Fixed-degree searches cannot cover that class at all
scales. Investigate growing-degree Boolean families with exploitable
character-sum structure, not only interpolation of already-known rows.

2. Change minimal supports, not just their weights.
On at mostN atoms the N-4-bit moment target forces uniformN. Failed
minimal supports therefore require replacement or enlargement.
Adaptive right-inverse correction on richer supports has an explicit
conditioning and positivity cost; these hypotheses must be proved for
any proposed near-extremal construction.

3. Global entropy optimization must bypass stable competing minima.
The complete four-bit Renyi diagram shows that changing entropy order
does not force the wrong local minimum to destabilize. The transition
is exactly characterized, but no general global continuation theorem
follows. Nonlocal moves or an independent global argument remain needed.

4. Lattice potentials need more than odd-coset shortness.
The successful explicit12-to16row augmentation increases the odd
kernel minimum from40toinfinity. Deletion alone trivially admits the
deleted row as a short kernel vector, which does not establish an
augmenting pair. Distinguish parity, vector length and compatibility
of several inserted sign rows.

5. Maintain scope and priority checks.
These are constructions at classical known orders, analytic benchmark
theorems, and conditional repair tools. They do not establish the
missing near-optimal distribution at an unknown order. Compare the
degree statements with Hadamard-code/Reed-Muller literature and the
entropy polytope diagram with convex entropy optimization before
claiming bibliographic novelty.
