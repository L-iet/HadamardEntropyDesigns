OPTIONAL ANALYTIC FIVE-ROW CLASSIFICATION

This reduction was derived alongside classify.py, but is not needed for
the main rough-order theorem. It explains the finite classification.
No bibliographic novelty has been established.

Take five orthogonal sign rows. Normalize the first to all ones by
column sign changes. The column distribution on four remaining signs x
is unbiased and pairwise independent, so its Fourier formula is
  w(x) = [1 + mu.v(x)]/16,
where v(x)=(products of the three signs omitting each coordinate,
product of all four signs). The sixteen v(x) are exactly the sign
vectors of length five with product +1.

The allowed mu-polytope is conv({+/-e_i} union {v/3:prod(v)=+1}).
An elementary explicit decomposition proves this without computer use:
set a_i=abs(mu_i). If product(sign(mu_i))=-1, the inequalities give
sum(a_i)<=1, so mu is in the unit cross-polytope. If the sign product
is +1, put b=min(a_i), r_i=a_i-b. The minimum of v.mu over even-parity
v is -sum(a_i)+2b; positivity says sum(r_i)+3b<=1. Hence
  mu = (3b)*(sign(mu)/3) + sum_i r_i sign(mu_i)e_i
        + [1-3b-sum(r_i)]*0.
Zero coordinates are handled by b=0; 0 is in the cross-polytope.

For a parity vertex +/-e_i, w is uniform on eight column types and
four of the five original rows form a closed quadruple. The fifth
row is independent. A sign linear form involving the independent fifth
row must be that row alone: for each fixed quadruple pattern, g+c and
g-c both have magnitude one, forcing gc=0. Otherwise the form lies
in the quadruple. Its fourth-moment identity gives
  sum_{i<j} c_i^2 c_j^2 = -6 delta*c_1*c_2*c_3*c_4.
AM-GM gives the reverse inequality in absolute value. Thus the form
is either a signed coordinate or has four coefficients of magnitude1/2,
with the coefficient sign product opposite the quadruple product delta.

For a vertex v/3, coordinate sign changes reduce its positive support
to bit weights 0,2,3 on four bits (eleven patterns). These patterns are
unisolvent for quadratic polynomials: a polynomial vanishing at zero
has zero constant; values at pairs give b_ij=-a_i-a_j; values at triples
give a_i+a_j+a_k=0, hence all a_i and b_ij vanish. Therefore if an affine
linear form f is a sign on this support, f^2-1 is identically zero on
the whole cube. Comparing its coefficients forces f to be a signed
coordinate, including the constant coordinate.

Any distribution decomposes into those vertex distributions, and each
positive vertex component has support inside the original support.
Consequently any genuinely new sign vector in the span of five
orthogonal sign rows has exactly four half-coefficients, on a closed
quadruple. It is also necessary that this same quadruple be closed on
the full column support, since a four-half-coefficient sum is a sign
precisely when that quadruple has the required constant product.

Among five orthogonal rows there is at most one closed quadruple:
two distinct quadruples share three rows and would force the remaining
two to be equal up to sign. If normalized first-column-one Hadamard
matrices differ in at most five row observations, every nontrivial new
row is one of the four rows supplied by J4/2-I4 on this quadruple.
The original and new quadruple bases have nonzero mutual inner products,
so they cannot be mixed in an orthogonal basis. Any fifth row is unchanged.
Thus a reduced trade of volume at most five is exactly a volume-four
closed-quadruple switch; volumes1,2,3,5 cannot occur.

At orders n>4, n=4 mod8 closed quadruples do not exist (field-sum
argument in rigidity.tex). Hence every reduced trade has volume at
least six. The explicit Paley12 six-row trade makes this sharp.

The code's 26-vertex enumeration and sign-form tests independently
check this fixed-dimensional analysis, but the reasoning above is
uniform in the number of columns.
