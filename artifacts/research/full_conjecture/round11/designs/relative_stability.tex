\section{Relative collision near-optimality does not give dimension-free stability}
\label{sec:design-relative-stability}
The next argument removes the earlier restriction $h>2(k-1)$.
Throughout, $h=2^s$, $2\le k\le h$, and $S$ is the full $k$-block source
support. The finite-field compressed laws remain exactly isotropic and
strict local collision maxima by the preceding character-space proof.
The coding inequality below is the classical Plotkin squared-sum argument;
its original source is M. Plotkin, \emph{Binary codes with specified
minimum distance} (1960), \url{https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.1960.1057584}.
The finite-field array input is classical. Novelty of these combined
stability statements has not been established.

\begin{proposition}[General support bound without a small-$k$ restriction]
\label{prop:design-plotkin-support}
For every $2\le k\le h$,
\[
 \omega(S)\le h\left\lfloor\frac{h}{h-k+1}\right\rfloor.
\]
Consequently any law supported on $S$ has distance from $\mathcal H_{kh}$
at least
$1-\frac1k\lfloor h/(h-k+1)\rfloor$, when this is positive.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Group an orthogonal row set by the value of $x_1$, giving $h$ groups.
For two rows in one group the kernel is $h c-2d$, where $c\ge1$ is
the number of equal block seeds and $d\le k-1<h$ is the distance
between their mixed-bit strings. Orthogonality forces $c=1$ and
$d=h/2$. The mixed sign strings have length $k-1$ and pairwise inner
product $-(h-k+1)$. If a group has $q$ rows, the squared norm of their
sum is $q(k-1)-q(q-1)(h-k+1)\ge0$, giving
$q\le h/(h-k+1)$. Sum over the $h$ groups and use the support witness.
\end{proof}

A stronger conclusion is available for a deliberately chosen compressed
support $U\subset S$. Take slopes $P\subset\mathbb F_h$, $0\in P$,
$|P|=k$, and use seeds $x_t=A+tB$ for $t\in P$. Denote the omitted
slopes by $R=\mathbb F_h\setminus P$.
\begin{theorem}[Spanning omitted slopes give the sharp distance]
\label{thm:design-spanning-slopes}
Assume $h/2<k<h$ and $R$ linearly spans the additive $s$-space
$\mathbb F_h$. Then the orthogonality graph of the $h^2$ compressed
augmented rows is exactly the disjoint union of $h$ cliques of size $h$,
one for each fixed $B$.
The uniform compressed law $\mu_P$ is a strict local collision maximum,
with $C(\mu_P)=1/h^2$.
If a Hadamard matrix of order $k$ exists, then the full global optimum
at order $n=kh$ is $1/(kh)$ and
\[
 \frac{C(\mu_P)}{C_{\rm global}}=\frac{k}{h},\qquad
 \inf_{\nu\in\mathcal H_{kh}}\|\mu_P-\nu\|_{\rm TV}=1-\frac1k.
\]
Such a slope set exists whenever $s\le h-k<h/2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For two seed pairs let $a=\Delta A$, $b=\Delta B$.
If $b=0$ and $a\ne0$, no block seeds agree and every mixed difference
vanishes, so their rows are orthogonal. If $b\ne0$, the equation
$a+tb=0$ holds for at most one observed slope, so the kernel is
$h c-2d$ with $c\in\{0,1\}$. The number of negative mixed differences is
\[
 d=\#\{t\in P\setminus\{0\}:e(tb)=1\}
   =h/2-\#\{t\in R:e(tb)=1\}.
\]
Spanning of $R$ and nonzero $e$ imply the last count is positive.
Since $|R|<h/2$, it is also less than $h/2$. Thus $0<d<h/2$ and
$hc-2d\ne0$. This proves the claimed graph and $\omega(U)=h$.
The strict local maximum follows from the same complementary pair of
block character spaces used in the concentration theorem; that proof
requires only distinct slopes, not $h>2(k-1)$.

The support witness gives the lower distance $1-1/k$. For each fixed
$B$, complete its $h$-clique by the previous block construction using
any normalized order-$k$ Hadamard matrix in place of Walsh. These $h$
completions remain disjoint: the first Walsh block recovers $A$, the
other blocks recover their sign multipliers and block seeds, and the
normalized order-$k$ rows are distinct. They meet $U$ only in their
own clique, since $\omega(U)=h$. Averaging the completed laws puts
mass $\mu_P/k$ on $U$, attaining the distance. Tensoring the order-$k$
Hadamard matrix with Walsh order $h$ gives the global endpoint, and
the atom bound gives global optimality. Finally, choose $R$ to contain
an additive basis and add arbitrary nonzero slopes until its size is
$h-k$; then $0\in P$ as required.
\end{proof}

For example, take $h=16$, $k=12$, and omit the polynomial-coordinate
basis slopes $R=\{1,2,4,8\}$. The resulting exactly isotropic law has
only $256$ atoms on $191$ bits, collision $1/256$ versus optimum $1/192$,
and distance exactly $11/12$ from every convex mixture of Hadamard laws.
It is nevertheless a strict local collision maximum. The checker uses
an original finite-field implementation modulo $x^4+x+1$ and the
classical Paley order-twelve matrix for block completion.

\begin{corollary}[A near-optimal relative regime]
\label{cor:design-relative-regime}
Suppose known Hadamard orders $k_i$ and powers of two $h_i$ satisfy
$k_i/h_i\to1$ and
$\log_2h_i\le h_i-k_i<h_i/2$.
Then there are exactly isotropic strict local maxima whose collision
divided by the global optimum tends to one, while their distance from
all Hadamard mixtures tends to one.
Their absolute collision deficit is
\[
 \frac1{k_i h_i}-\frac1{h_i^2}
 =\frac{h_i-k_i}{k_i h_i^2}.
\]
\end{corollary}
This is a relative-stability obstruction, not a contradiction of
sufficiently small dimension-dependent absolute collision windows.
Existence of a suitable infinite sequence of known orders can be
supplied separately by Paley orders and irrational-rotation density.
The theorem itself constructs laws only at already-known Hadamard orders.
