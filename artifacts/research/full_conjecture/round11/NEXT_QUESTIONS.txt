NEXT RESEARCH QUESTIONS

1. Improve counts, not just compatibility.
The quotient grid provides an exact optimum when the remaining
orthogonal quotient has dimensionfour, and global moments provide
a certificate in higher dimension. Find retained spaces with
favorable grid weights or moment inequalities at unknown orders.
Neither structure alone guarantees a near-complete frame.

2. Replace uniform random neighbor steps with a proved guided rule.
The full known-order census has only42 improving neighbors among
2441406. The exact walk drift lowers above-mean sign counts in
expectation. A guided all-order rule needs a guaranteed improving
choice or a controlled route through equal/worse counts.
One finite census does not exclude other suboptimal local maxima.

3. Seek global constructions beyond decomposition into Hadamard blocks.
Even relative collision approaching its optimum and vanishing additive
entropy excess do not give dimension-independent closeness to any
Hadamard mixture. The new spanning-omitted-slopes construction proves
this with EXACT moments at known orders, not approximate examples.
Any successful near-extremal argument needs dimension dependence or
additional structural assumptions; it cannot rely on relative error alone.
The exact isotropic source family is distance1-1/k from every
Hadamard mixture, with a sharp nearly-total row replacement budget.
This defeats a universal small-repair principle but leaves
unrestricted global constructions and higher-dimensional models open.
Its n-1-bit target must not be conflated with the N-4-bit extraction law.

4. Explore higher completion moments or quotient geometry at rank>=3.
The second-moment certificate succeeds on one retained-space H20
example and fails on a weaker one. Higher moments depend on isotropic
span ranks of tuples; any usefulness needs actual quantitative data
or uniform bounds, not just a formal expansion.

Novelty, all-order positivity, and the full conjecture remain open here.
