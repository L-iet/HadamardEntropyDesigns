\begin{abstract}
We continue three complementary approaches to Hadamard existence.
At infinitely many known Hadamard orders, exactly pairwise-independent
laws can have relatively optimal collision and vanishing additive
entropy excess, yet distance tending to one from every convex mixture
of Hadamard laws. The laws are strict local collision maxima.
The finite-field construction has exact distance $1-1/k$.
An explicit global repair attains the sharp replacement fraction
$1-1/k$ while preserving endpoint collision probability in a separate
divisible power-of-two parameter regime.
Second, a four-dimensional isotropic quotient turns all compatible
completions into weighted rows and columns of a finite grid.
Third, exact global completion moments give a quantitative count
certificate, while a census of all 2441406 neighbors of one specified
order-twenty code finds exactly 42 improving moves.
These are structural theorems, constructive known-order benchmarks,
and finite certificates. They neither prove a count surplus at
unknown orders nor resolve the Hadamard conjecture. Novelty remains
unverified; classical incidence, code and character ingredients are
credited.
\end{abstract}
The standing near-extremal distribution target and its proven
extraction implication are unchanged; see
\path{../round6/collision_stability.tex}. We do not assume this input
exists at every admissible order. The repair family below concerns
full $n-1$-bit isotropy at known power-of-two orders, whereas the
extraction input is punctured to $N-4$ bits.
\section{Isotropic laws can be almost maximally far from Hadamard mixtures}
\label{sec:design-hadamard-distance}
A Hadamard law of order $n$ is the uniform law on the $n$ rows of a
sign Hadamard matrix whose first column is all positive, after deleting
that column. Let $\mathcal H_n$ be the convex hull of all such laws on
$\{-1,1\}^{n-1}$. This section gives exact balanced pairwise-independent
laws whose total variation distance from $\mathcal H_n$ tends to one,
at orders where Hadamard matrices already exist. It also determines
an exact global observation-trade budget for conversion into a disjoint
union of Hadamard blocks. Thus a hypothetical procedure converting
arbitrary isotropic laws into Hadamard mixtures cannot generally make
only a small change.

Orthogonal arrays, row multiplicities, and Rao-type simplicity arguments
are established topics; a primary reference consulted here is
Carlet--Kiss--Nagy, \emph{Simplicity conditions for binary orthogonal arrays},
\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.00835}. Our elementary character and
block-Hadamard ingredients are classical. Priority for the quantitative
distance and repair statements is not asserted.

For a support $S$ of sign-bit vectors let $\omega(S)$ be the maximum
size of a subset whose augmented rows $(1,x)$ are mutually orthogonal.
\begin{lemma}[A support witness against all Hadamard mixtures]
\label{lem:design-support-witness}
If $\mu$ is any probability measure supported on $S$, then
\[
 \inf_{\nu\in\mathcal H_n}\|\mu-\nu\|_{\rm TV}
 \ge1-\frac{\omega(S)}n.
\]
If a multiset of $D$ original rows is supported on $S$ and $n\mid D$,
conversion to a union of $D/n$ order-$n$ Hadamard blocks requires at
least $D(1-\omega(S)/n)$ observation replacements.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each Hadamard block has at most $\omega(S)$ rows in $S$, hence every
mixture gives $\nu(S)\le\omega(S)/n$. Use
$\|\mu-\nu\|_{\rm TV}\ge\mu(S)-\nu(S)$. A union of $D/n$ blocks
can retain at most $(D/n)\omega(S)$ original observations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Sharp distance and global repair family]
\label{thm:design-sharp-distance}
Let $k,h$ be powers of two, $k\ge2$ and $h>2(k-1)$. Put
$n=kh$ and $D=h^k$. There is an explicit uniform $D$-atom law $\mu$
on $n-1$ unbiased pairwise-independent bits for which
\[
 \inf_{\nu\in\mathcal H_n}\|\mu-\nu\|_{\rm TV}=1-\frac1k.
\]
Moreover the minimum number of observation replacements converting
its $D$ distinct rows into a union of $D/n$ Hadamard blocks is exactly
$D(1-1/k)$. Such a conversion can preserve distinctness of every row,
so its endpoint has the same collision probability $1/D$ as $\mu$.
Taking $h=2k$ and $k\to\infty$ gives distances tending to one.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $h=2^s$ and $V=\mathbb F_2^s$. Fix nonzero $e\in V^*$.
Take independent uniform seeds $x_1,\ldots,x_k\in V$ and observe
\[
 (\chi_\alpha(x_j))_{1\le j\le k,\ \alpha\ne0},\qquad
 (\chi_e(x_1+x_j))_{2\le j\le k},
 \quad\chi_\alpha(x)=(-1)^{\alpha(x)}.
\]
There are $k(h-1)+(k-1)=n-1$ bits, all distinct nonzero seed
characters, so they are unbiased and pairwise independent. All block
characters determine their seeds, giving $D=h^k$ distinct atoms.

For two augmented rows corresponding to $x$ and $y$, their dot product is
\[
 h\#\{j:x_j=y_j\}+1-k+
 \sum_{j=2}^k(-1)^{e(x_1+x_j+y_1+y_j)}.
\]
If they share any block seed, this is at least $h-2(k-1)>0$.
Therefore any orthogonal set has all $x_1$ values distinct and size
at most $h$. Conversely, for fixed offsets $c_2,\ldots,c_k$, the $h$
seeds $x_1=a$, $x_j=a+c_j$ are mutually orthogonal: every mixed bit
is constant along the set. These offset cliques partition the entire
support. Thus $\omega(S)=h$, proving the lower bounds.

We complete every offset clique explicitly. Arrange its augmented
columns into $k$ blocks of length $h$: the first block is
$(1,\chi_\alpha(x_1))$, and the $j$th is
$(\chi_e(x_1+x_j),\chi_\alpha(x_j))$. On the clique these blocks
are $h\times h$ Hadamard matrices $H_1,\ldots,H_k$:
$H_1$ is Walsh and each other block is Walsh with permuted rows and
possibly a negated constant column. Let $K$ be the normalized Walsh
matrix of order $k$. The $kh$ rows indexed by $(a,t)$, with blocks
\[
 K_{t1}H_1(a),\ldots,K_{tk}H_k(a),
\]
form a Hadamard matrix. Indeed rows with different $a$ have zero inner
product separately in every block; rows with the same $a$ have dot
product $h\sum_jK_{tj}K_{t'j}=kh\mathbf1_{t=t'}$.
Its constant augmented column is positive because $K_{t1}=1$.
The first Walsh row of $K$ recovers the original clique. No other row
belongs to $S$, since that would exceed $\omega(S)=h$.

Completions of distinct offset cliques have disjoint supports. The first
block determines $a$. In every other block the nonconstant coordinates
have form $\sigma\chi_\alpha(b)$; for $h\ge4$ they determine both
$\sigma$ and $b$ uniquely. Otherwise a nontrivial character would equal
$-1$ at every nonzero frequency, contradicted by multiplying the values
at two distinct frequencies and comparing their sum. The signs determine
the Walsh row $t$, and the seeds determine the offsets $c_j=b_j-a$.

Averaging all $h^{k-1}$ completed Hadamard laws assigns mass
$1/(kD)$ to each original source row, namely $\mu/k$, and puts the
remaining mass outside $S$. Its total variation distance is exactly
$1-1/k$, proving sharpness. Finally choose any
$D/n=h^{k-1}/k$ offset cliques and take the union of their completions.
This integer exists since $k,h$ are powers of two and $h\ge2k$.
The union has $D$ distinct rows and exactly $D/k$ original rows, achieving
the observation-replacement lower bound. Both endpoint arrays have
$D$ distinct equal-weight atoms and collision $1/D$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Consequences and scope.}
The initial and final laws have identical first and second moments and
identical collision probability, but only the final array is exhibited
as a union of Hadamard blocks. The construction therefore supplies an
exact large global trade, while proving that every such repair changes
at least the stated fraction of observations. The theorem uses known
power-of-two block matrices and does not produce a missing Hadamard
order. For $k>2$ we make no local-optimality claim about the source law.

\paragraph{A denominator caution.}
For a rational pairwise-independent law on $n-1$ bits written with
common denominator $D$, the standard atom bound gives integer
multiplicities at most $\lfloor D/n\rfloor$. Consequently
\[
 C\le\frac{\lfloor D/n\rfloor}{D}
 =\frac1n-\frac{D\bmod n}{nD}.
\]
In particular a nonuniform rational law requires $D\ge2n$.
This is a simplicity/denominator restriction, not a new existence
criterion. It refers to $n-1$ bits. For the punctured $N-4$-bit extraction
input the corresponding atom bound is only $1/(N-3)$; collision at most
$1/N$ does not justify replacing that atom bound by $1/N$.
\section{Optimal concentration on the same support is still a poor global solution}
\label{sec:design-concentration}
Keep the source support $S$ from Theorem~\ref{thm:design-sharp-distance}:
$k$ independent seed blocks in $V=\mathbb F_2^s$, $h=2^s$, all their
nonzero additive characters, and the $k-1$ mixed bits
$\chi_e(x_1+x_j)$. This section optimizes collision over \emph{all}
exactly isotropic weights on that support, rather than just its original
uniform $h^k$-atom law. The finite-field array used for attainment is a
classical index-one orthogonal-array construction; see K. A. Bush,
\emph{Orthogonal Arrays of Index Unity} (1952),
\url{https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177729387}. It is credited as an
input, not a new design or Hadamard construction. Priority for the
combined concentration and distance statements is unestablished.

\begin{theorem}[Exact fixed-support collision optimum]
\label{thm:design-face-optimum}
For $2\le k\le h$, every unbiased pairwise-independent law supported
on $S$ has atom masses at most $1/h^2$ and collision at most $1/h^2$.
The upper bound is attained. Every attaining law is uniform on $h^2$
atoms; its projection onto any two seed blocks is a bijection onto
$V^2$. Thus its seed rows form an $\mathrm{OA}(h^2,k,h,2)$, equivalently
an index-one strength-two array (with additional mixed-bit constraints).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
All nonzero additive characters of block $i$ are observed. Means and
cross-pair moments therefore give
$\mathbb E\chi_\alpha(x_i)\chi_\beta(x_j)=0$ whenever
$(\alpha,\beta)\ne(0,0)$. Fourier inversion says the block pair
$(x_i,x_j)$ is uniform on $V^2$. An individual full seed atom has
mass at most its block-pair marginal $1/h^2$, so
$C=\sum w_x^2\le1/h^2$. Equality forces every positive weight to be
$1/h^2$, and pairwise uniformity then forces each block-pair projection
of its $h^2$ rows to be bijective.

To attain the bound, identify $V$ with the additive group of
$\mathbb F_h$, choose distinct slopes $t_1,\ldots,t_k\in\mathbb F_h$,
and set $x_j=A+t_jB$ for uniform independent $A,B\in\mathbb F_h$.
All block characters are distinct nonzero linear forms in $(A,B)$:
if $\alpha(A+t_iB)=\beta(A+t_jB)$, comparison of the $A$ terms gives
$\alpha=\beta$; for $i\ne j$, invertibility of multiplication by
$t_i+t_j$ then forces $\alpha=0$, excluded. The mixed characters are
$e((t_1+t_j)B)$; they are distinct nonzero forms with zero $A$ part,
so they differ from all block forms as well. Every observed bit is
therefore a distinct nonzero character of the two-block seed. The
resulting law is unbiased and pairwise independent. Two block seeds
recover $(A,B)$, so its support has $h^2$ distinct atoms.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Simultaneous face optimum, strict trap, and sharp distance]
\label{thm:design-concentrated-trap}
Now let $k,h$ be powers of two with $h>2(k-1)$ and $n=kh$.
The finite-field law $\mu_*$ above is a global collision maximizer among
isotropic laws supported on $S$, and a strict local collision maximum
in the \emph{full} pairwise-independent law polytope on $n-1$ bits.
Its collision is $1/h^2$, while the full global optimum is $1/(kh)$.
Furthermore
\[
 \inf_{\nu\in\mathcal H_n}\|\mu_*-\nu\|_{\rm TV}=1-1/k.
\]
Exactly $h^2(1-1/k)$ observation replacements suffice and are necessary
to turn its $h^2$ distinct rows into $h/k$ Hadamard blocks, while keeping
collision $1/h^2$. Taking $h=k^2$ makes the local-to-global collision
ratio $1/k\to0$ simultaneously with distance $1-1/k\to1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The fixed-support maximum was just proved. For strict local maximality,
consider any two distinct slopes. Their block character spaces are
complementary $s$-dimensional subspaces of the $2s$-dimensional seed
dual: their intersection is zero by the same slope argument. Every
character of $(A,B)$ is consequently a constant, observed bit, or product
of two observed bits. These degree-at-most-two real monomials span all
functions on the $h^2$-atom support $U$ of $\mu_*$. Exact first and second
moments therefore determine every weight on $U$ uniquely.
A uniform measure with this property strictly exposes itself by its
own gradient: for feasible $q$,
$\mu_*\cdot(q-\mu_*)=-q(U^c)/h^2$, with equality only at $q=\mu_*$.
A strictly exposed vertex of a finite polytope is a strict local maximum
of the squared Euclidean norm: the negative linear directional term
dominates its quadratic increment in a sufficiently small neighborhood.
A quantitative version follows below.

The universal support witness gives distance at least $1-1/k$ because
$U\subset S$ and $\omega(S)=h$. For each fixed $B=b$, the $h$ points
$x_j=A+t_jb$ form one offset clique from the preceding completion
construction. The $h$ such cliques partition $U$. Complete them all by
Walsh block mixing. Their completions are disjoint, each intersects $U$
in its own $h$-clique, and averaging their Hadamard laws gives mass
$\mu_*/k$ on $U$. This attains distance $1-1/k$ exactly. Taking any
$h/k$ cliques and the union of their completions retains $h^2/k$ original
rows out of $h^2$, attains the replacement lower bound, and keeps all
rows distinct. The normalized Walsh order-$n$ matrix attains global
collision $1/n$, which the standard atom bound shows is optimal.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[A certified local barrier]
\label{prop:design-concentrated-barrier}
Put $m=h^2$ and $b=q(U^c)$ for a feasible competing law $q$. Then
\[
 C(q)-1/m\le-2b/m+(m+1)b^2.
\]
Hence every $0<b<2/[m(m+1)]$ strictly lowers collision, and every
continuous feasible path from $\mu_*$ to a better law passes through
collision at most $1/m-1/[m^2(m+1)]$. This barrier is not claimed optimal.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Represent each seed character $\chi$ by a degree-at-most-two observable
monomial $\psi_\chi$ using the complementary block spaces. The polynomials
\[
 F_u=\frac1m\sum_\chi\chi(u)\psi_\chi
\]
restrict to indicators of individual atoms on $U$ and satisfy
$|F_u|\le1$ on the entire observable cube. Fixed moments give
$q(u)+\mathbb E_q[\mathbf1_{U^c}F_u]=1/m$, so
$|q(u)-1/m|\le b$. Thus
$\|q-\mu_*\|_2^2\le mb^2+b^2$, while
$\mu_*\cdot(q-\mu_*)=-b/m$. Expanding collision proves the inequality.
Any better endpoint must have $b\ge2/[m(m+1)]$; continuity crosses
$b=1/[m(m+1)]$, where the displayed collision loss is $1/[m^2(m+1)]$.
\end{proof}

These results show that even globally optimizing collision on a large,
explicit, exactly isotropic support can leave an arbitrarily poor strict
local solution that is almost maximally far from all Hadamard mixtures.
The finite-field attainment theorem solves that support face completely;
it does not solve the original all-order Hadamard existence problem.

\begin{corollary}[Moderate-support regimes]
\label{cor:design-moderate-support}
Taking $h=2k$ gives $n=2k^2$ and a strict local maximum on just
$m=h^2=2n$ atoms, with collision $1/(2n)$ and exact distance
$1-\sqrt{2/n}$ from $\mathcal H_n$. Taking $h=k^2$ instead gives
$n=k^3$, $m=k^4=n^{4/3}$, collision-to-global ratio $n^{-1/3}$,
and exact distance $1-n^{-1/3}$ from $\mathcal H_n$.
\end{corollary}
\section{Relative collision near-optimality does not give dimension-free stability}
\label{sec:design-relative-stability}
The next argument removes the earlier restriction $h>2(k-1)$.
Throughout, $h=2^s$, $2\le k\le h$, and $S$ is the full $k$-block source
support. The finite-field compressed laws remain exactly isotropic and
strict local collision maxima by the preceding character-space proof.
The coding inequality below is the classical Plotkin squared-sum argument;
its original source is M. Plotkin, \emph{Binary codes with specified
minimum distance} (1960), \url{https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.1960.1057584}.
The finite-field array input is classical. Novelty of these combined
stability statements has not been established.

\begin{proposition}[General support bound without a small-$k$ restriction]
\label{prop:design-plotkin-support}
For every $2\le k\le h$,
\[
 \omega(S)\le h\left\lfloor\frac{h}{h-k+1}\right\rfloor.
\]
Consequently any law supported on $S$ has distance from $\mathcal H_{kh}$
at least
$1-\frac1k\lfloor h/(h-k+1)\rfloor$, when this is positive.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Group an orthogonal row set by the value of $x_1$, giving $h$ groups.
For two rows in one group the kernel is $h c-2d$, where $c\ge1$ is
the number of equal block seeds and $d\le k-1<h$ is the distance
between their mixed-bit strings. Orthogonality forces $c=1$ and
$d=h/2$. The mixed sign strings have length $k-1$ and pairwise inner
product $-(h-k+1)$. If a group has $q$ rows, the squared norm of their
sum is $q(k-1)-q(q-1)(h-k+1)\ge0$, giving
$q\le h/(h-k+1)$. Sum over the $h$ groups and use the support witness.
\end{proof}

A stronger conclusion is available for a deliberately chosen compressed
support $U\subset S$. Take slopes $P\subset\mathbb F_h$, $0\in P$,
$|P|=k$, and use seeds $x_t=A+tB$ for $t\in P$. Denote the omitted
slopes by $R=\mathbb F_h\setminus P$.
\begin{theorem}[Spanning omitted slopes give the sharp distance]
\label{thm:design-spanning-slopes}
Assume $h/2<k<h$ and $R$ linearly spans the additive $s$-space
$\mathbb F_h$. Then the orthogonality graph of the $h^2$ compressed
augmented rows is exactly the disjoint union of $h$ cliques of size $h$,
one for each fixed $B$.
The uniform compressed law $\mu_P$ is a strict local collision maximum,
with $C(\mu_P)=1/h^2$.
If a Hadamard matrix of order $k$ exists, then the full global optimum
at order $n=kh$ is $1/(kh)$ and
\[
 \frac{C(\mu_P)}{C_{\rm global}}=\frac{k}{h},\qquad
 \inf_{\nu\in\mathcal H_{kh}}\|\mu_P-\nu\|_{\rm TV}=1-\frac1k.
\]
Such a slope set exists whenever $s\le h-k<h/2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For two seed pairs let $a=\Delta A$, $b=\Delta B$.
If $b=0$ and $a\ne0$, no block seeds agree and every mixed difference
vanishes, so their rows are orthogonal. If $b\ne0$, the equation
$a+tb=0$ holds for at most one observed slope, so the kernel is
$h c-2d$ with $c\in\{0,1\}$. The number of negative mixed differences is
\[
 d=\#\{t\in P\setminus\{0\}:e(tb)=1\}
   =h/2-\#\{t\in R:e(tb)=1\}.
\]
Spanning of $R$ and nonzero $e$ imply the last count is positive.
Since $|R|<h/2$, it is also less than $h/2$. Thus $0<d<h/2$ and
$hc-2d\ne0$. This proves the claimed graph and $\omega(U)=h$.
The strict local maximum follows from the same complementary pair of
block character spaces used in the concentration theorem; that proof
requires only distinct slopes, not $h>2(k-1)$.

The support witness gives the lower distance $1-1/k$. For each fixed
$B$, complete its $h$-clique by the previous block construction using
any normalized order-$k$ Hadamard matrix in place of Walsh. These $h$
completions remain disjoint: the first Walsh block recovers $A$, the
other blocks recover their sign multipliers and block seeds, and the
normalized order-$k$ rows are distinct. They meet $U$ only in their
own clique, since $\omega(U)=h$. Averaging the completed laws puts
mass $\mu_P/k$ on $U$, attaining the distance. Tensoring the order-$k$
Hadamard matrix with Walsh order $h$ gives the global endpoint, and
the atom bound gives global optimality. Finally, choose $R$ to contain
an additive basis and add arbitrary nonzero slopes until its size is
$h-k$; then $0\in P$ as required.
\end{proof}

For example, take $h=16$, $k=12$, and omit the polynomial-coordinate
basis slopes $R=\{1,2,4,8\}$. The resulting exactly isotropic law has
only $256$ atoms on $191$ bits, collision $1/256$ versus optimum $1/192$,
and distance exactly $11/12$ from every convex mixture of Hadamard laws.
It is nevertheless a strict local collision maximum. The checker uses
an original finite-field implementation modulo $x^4+x+1$ and the
classical Paley order-twelve matrix for block completion.

\begin{corollary}[A near-optimal relative regime]
\label{cor:design-relative-regime}
Suppose known Hadamard orders $k_i$ and powers of two $h_i$ satisfy
$k_i/h_i\to1$ and
$\log_2h_i\le h_i-k_i<h_i/2$.
Then there are exactly isotropic strict local maxima whose collision
divided by the global optimum tends to one, while their distance from
all Hadamard mixtures tends to one.
Their absolute collision deficit is
\[
 \frac1{k_i h_i}-\frac1{h_i^2}
 =\frac{h_i-k_i}{k_i h_i^2}.
\]
\end{corollary}
This is a relative-stability obstruction, not a contradiction of
sufficiently small dimension-dependent absolute collision windows.
Existence of a suitable infinite sequence of known orders can be
supplied separately by Paley orders and irrational-rotation density.
The theorem itself constructs laws only at already-known Hadamard orders.
\section{Near-optimal collision and entropy can coexist with maximal distance}
\label{sec:relative-sequence}

The omitted-slope theorem gives a particularly strong stability
counterexample at infinitely many \emph{known} Hadamard orders.
The existence ingredients are classical: Paley's construction
(\emph{On Orthogonal Matrices}, 1933,
\url{https://doi.org/10.1002/sapm1933121311}) and Sylvester tensoring.
No bibliographic novelty is asserted for the resulting application.

\begin{theorem}[No dimension-independent stability, even for exact moments]
\label{thm:relative-no-stability}
There are orders $n_j$ at which Hadamard matrices exist and exactly
unbiased pairwise-independent laws $\mu_j$ on $n_j-1$ sign bits such that
\[
 n_j C(\mu_j)\longrightarrow1,\qquad
 \inf_{\nu\in\mathcal H_{n_j}}\|\mu_j-\nu\|_{\rm TV}
 \longrightarrow1.
\]
The laws can be chosen to be uniform vertices and strict local
collision maxima of the full moment polytope. Moreover, for every
R\'enyi order $\alpha>0$, including Shannon and min-entropy by their
limiting conventions,
\[
 H_\alpha(\mu_j)-\min_{\lambda\ {\rm isotropic}}H_\alpha(\lambda)
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
The same sequence works for all these entropy orders.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We choose powers of two $h=2^s$ and known Hadamard orders $k$ with
\[
 k/h\to1,\quad k\to\infty,\quad
 s\le g:=h-k<h/2.
\]
In $\mathbb F_h$, choose $g$ nonzero omitted slopes containing an
additive basis, so their complement has $k$ slopes and contains zero.
The preceding theorem constructs the uniform law on $h^2$ atoms,
with exact moments, strict local maximality, and
\[
 n=kh,\qquad C(\mu)=h^{-2},\qquad
 \inf_{\nu\in\mathcal H_n}\|\mu-\nu\|_{\rm TV}=1-\frac1k.
\]
An $H_k$ tensor a Sylvester $H_h$ gives an $H_n$, so the comparison
class is nonempty. The relative collision ratio is $n/h^2=k/h$.

For any isotropic law on $n-1$ bits, let $v_x=(1,x)$.
Since $\sum_xw_xv_xv_x^T=I_n$, each positive rank-one summand
is bounded by $I_n$, giving $nw_x\le1$.
Every R\'enyi entropy is therefore at least $\log n$.
A Hadamard law attains this value. The uniform $h^2$-atom law has
all its R\'enyi entropies equal to $2\log h$, and its common excess is
\[
                  2\log h-\log(kh)=\log(h/k)\to0.
\]

It remains to justify the simultaneous choices without an unproved
prime-pattern assumption. Put $q=3^{2u+1}$ and $k=q+1$; Paley gives
an $H_k$ since $q\equiv3\pmod4$. Let
$h=2^{\lceil\log_2 q\rceil}$. The fractional parts of
$(2u+1)\log_2 3$ are dense modulo one, because the increment
$\log_2 9$ is irrational. Thus for each integer $t\ge4$ we may choose
an arbitrarily large, increasing $u$ for which
\[
       1-\frac2t<\frac qh<1-\frac1t.
\]
In particular choose $h\ge t^3$ and
$g=h-q-1\ge\log_2h$. The latter follows for all sufficiently large
hits since $h-q>h/t$ and $h/\log h\to\infty$.
Then $0<g<h/2$, $g\ge s$, $k/h\to1$, and $k\to\infty$.
Density of irrational rotations is elementary: pigeonhole finitely
many orbit points to obtain an arbitrarily small nonzero rotation
step; its multiples form a fine net. The same argument applies
after discarding any finite prefix, ensuring arbitrarily large hits.
No assumption on infinitely many Mersenne primes, or other special
prime patterns, enters the proof.
\end{proof}

Equivalently, for any $\epsilon,\eta>0$ there is a known Hadamard
order $n$ and an exact isotropic law with
$C\ge(1-\epsilon)/n$ but distance at least $1-\eta$ from
\emph{every convex mixture} of order-$n$ Hadamard laws.
Consequently no dimension-independent modulus tending to zero can
bound this distance from relative collision error, nor from additive
Shannon-entropy excess. This is an instability theorem across
dimensions, not a failure of compactness or stability at fixed $n$.

The laws here have $n-1$ bits. They must not be identified with the
$N-4$-bit input of the earlier extraction theorem. Their absolute
deficit is $(h-k)/(kh^2)$; no sufficiently small fixed-order deficit
is asserted. Nor do we claim that the $h^2$ observations can be
partitioned into order-$kh$ blocks with unchanged collision:
the Paley multiplier $k$ generally does not divide $h$. The distance
upper bound uses a convex average, which needs no such divisibility.
\section{Codimension-two repair is a weighted grid problem}
\label{sec:quotient-grid}

Let $p$ be an odd prime, $N=4p$, and $m=N/2$. Fix a self-dual code
$C\subseteq\mathbb F_p^N$ and a subspace $G\subseteq C$ of dimension
$m-2$. Assume $G$ contains at least five orthogonal sign classes.
Write $b=|S(G)|$ and let $\mathcal P$ be the set of normalized sign
classes whose residues lie in $G^\perp$, with $M=|\mathcal P|$.
The four-dimensional quotient $G^\perp/G$ converts compatibility
search into an explicit weighted grid.

The underlying two-ruling geometry is classical: the split hyperbolic
quadric $Q^+(3,p)$ has $(p+1)^2$ points on two systems of $p+1$
generator lines. For a primary research source stating this structure,
see Edoukou, \emph{The weight distribution of the functional codes
defined by forms of degree 2 on Hermitian surfaces}, Theorem 4.2,
\url{https://www.numdam.org/article/JTNB_2009__21_1_131_0.pdf}.
We give the matrix proof needed here rather than relying on a
classification implementation. No novelty claim is made for the
finite geometry or the resulting reformulation.

\begin{theorem}[Weighted-grid classification]
The self-dual codes containing $G$ correspond to the $2(p+1)$ rows
and columns of a $(p+1)\times(p+1)$ grid. There are nonnegative
integer point weights $w_{ij}$ such that
\[
 M=b+\sum_{ij}w_{ij},\qquad
 |S(C_{\mathrm{row}\ i})|=b+\sum_jw_{ij},\qquad
 |S(C_{\mathrm{col}\ j})|=b+\sum_iw_{ij}.
\]
Every sign class outside $G$ is counted at exactly one grid point,
and belongs to exactly two of these codes. The classes in $G$ belong
to every code.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The quotient $G^\perp/G$ is nondegenerate of dimension four and has
the two-dimensional totally isotropic subspace $C/G$. Choose a
hyperbolic basis $e_1,e_2,f_1,f_2$, with $e_i\cdot f_j=\delta_{ij}$
and all other basis pairings zero. For explicit construction, choose
dual vectors $g_i$ to the $e_i$ and replace
$g_i$ by $f_i=g_i-\frac12\sum_j(g_i\cdot g_j)e_j$.
The quotient norm of $x_1e_1+x_2e_2+y_1f_1+y_2f_2$ is
$2(x_1y_1+x_2y_2)$. Identify this vector with
\[
 A=\begin{pmatrix}x_1&x_2\\-y_2&y_1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \tfrac12\|\bar v\|^2=\det A.
\]
A nonzero isotropic vector is a rank-one matrix $uv^{\mathsf T}$.
Its projective class is determined by a pair
$([u],[v])\in\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_p)^2$, giving the grid.
The totally isotropic planes are precisely the matrices with fixed
column line $[u]$ or fixed row line $[v]$. Indeed two independent
rank-one matrices span a rank-one plane only when their column lines
or their row lines agree. These are the two rulings, each of size $p+1$.
Their inverse images in $G^\perp$ are exactly the self-dual codes
containing $G$.

Every sign vector has norm $N=0$ in $\mathbb F_p$, so its quotient
is zero or isotropic. The zero quotient contributes $b$ common
classes. At each nonzero projective quotient point count its sign
classes as $w_{ij}$. Membership in the two ruling planes proves
all the displayed formulas.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact compatibility and optimal retained-space repair]
Every pairwise orthogonal subfamily of $\mathcal P$ lies in the
sign set of one of the $2(p+1)$ codes above. Consequently the maximum
size of a real orthogonal sign frame preserving the prescribed
retained rows spanning $G$ is exactly
\[
 \boxed{b+\max\left\{\max_i\sum_jw_{ij},\ \max_j\sum_iw_{ij}\right\}.}
\]
Thus full completion with this retained space is equivalent to a
grid line of weight $N-b$, and finding the optimum requires only
row and column sums after the sign pool has been computed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
All pool classes have the same sign parity as the five retained
orthogonal rows: an opposite-parity class would have inner-product
magnitude $N/2$ against all five, contradicting Bessel. Thus for
distinct pool classes real orthogonality is equivalent to orthogonality
modulo $p$. In the quotient, two rank-one matrices $uv^{\mathsf T}$
and $st^{\mathsf T}$ are orthogonal precisely when their column
lines or their row lines agree. This follows by polarizing the
determinant: its cross term is, up to the fixed convention,
$\det(u,s)\det(v,t)$.

Classes projecting to the same point are mutually compatible;
common $G$ classes are compatible with everything. A family of grid
points pairwise sharing a row or a column lies in one row or one
column: once two distinct points share a row, a point outside that
row cannot share both of their different columns. The one-point
case is immediate. Thus every compatible family lies in a ruling
plane. Conversely all sign classes in a ruling-plane code form
a real orthogonal frame, because that self-orthogonal code contains
the same five retained rows. Including all common classes proves
the exact maximum and constructively attains it.
\end{proof}

Put $W=M-b$. Double counting the grid gives
\[
 \sum_{D\supseteq G,\ D=D^\perp}|S(D)|=2M+2pb,
 \qquad
 \max_D|S(D)|\ge b+\left\lceil\frac{W}{p+1}\right\rceil.
\]
Also every ruling-line weight is at most $N-b$, hence
$W\le(p+1)(N-b)$. These are exact incidence constraints, not a
guarantee that some line reaches the maximum $N-b$.

There is a similarly explicit row-deletion objective. Let an initial
frame be the full sign set of one line $L_0$, with size $r_0$.
For any other line $L$, put $r_L=b+\sum_{x\in L}w_x$ and
$a_L=|S(C_{L_0})\cap S(C_L)|$. Then $a_L=b$ for a different line
of the same ruling, while $a_L=b+w_x$ for the crossing point $x$
of opposite-ruling lines. The minimum deletion count for an
augmenting exchange preserving $G$ is
\[
 \min_{L:r_L>r_0}(r_0-a_L),
\]
with value infinity if no qualifying line exists. Requiring
$r_L=N$ instead gives the minimum full-completion deletion count.
The compatibility theorem proves the lower bound, and replacing
the old classes outside the intersection by the new line's classes
attains it. Common classes never need to be sacrificed.

The checker embeds the earlier twelve-sign self-dual code over

lexicographically. The first retained choice uses sorted indices
$0,1,\ldots,7$. It gives $b=8$, $M=48$, and grid weights
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
0&2&1&0&0&1\\1&2&1&2&2&4\\2&4&2&1&1&2\\
1&2&0&0&0&1\\0&1&0&1&0&2\\0&1&0&0&1&2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The row sums are $(4,12,12,4,4,4)$ and column sums

The average lower bound gives only fifteen, whereas the exact

The original code is the first row line. The full second column

In both cases the twelve reconstructed self-dual bases, their exact

in the pool and every output Gram matrix is checked as an integer.
The examples demonstrate exact repair through quotient geometry at
a known order; an all-order theorem forcing enough concentration
on a quotient generator has not been proved.
\section{Global completion moments and the drift of an unguided code walk}
\label{sec:completion-moments}

Let $p$ be an odd prime, $N=4p$, and let $G$ be a totally isotropic
subspace of $\mathbb F_p^N$ contained in a self-dual code. Write
$\dim G=N/2-h$, where $h\ge2$. The quotient
$V=G^\perp/G$ is a split nondegenerate orthogonal space of dimension
$2h$. A sign class is normalized by its first coordinate being $+1$.
Every sign vector in $G^\perp$ is isotropic modulo $p$ because its
norm is $N$. Let $b$ count sign classes in $G$ itself, and assign
weight $w_\ell$ to each isotropic projective point $\ell$ of $V$:
it is the number of sign classes whose nonzero quotient lies on
$\ell$. Put
\[
 W=\sum_\ell w_\ell,\qquad S_2=\sum_\ell w_\ell^2,\qquad
 T=\sum_{\substack{\ell\ne k\\\ell\perp k}}w_\ell w_k.
\]
The last sum is ordered. These definitions count actual sign classes,
not arbitrary vectors of the finite-field code.

\begin{theorem}[Exact first and second completion moments]
\label{mom:two}
Choose uniformly among all self-dual codes $C$ containing $G$ and
put $Y=|S(C)|-b$. With $D_1=p^{h-1}+1$ and $D_2=p^{h-2}+1$,
\[
 \mathbb E Y=\frac{W}{D_1},\qquad
 \mathbb E Y^2=\frac{S_2}{D_1}+\frac{T}{D_1D_2}.
\]
If $W>0$, at least one such code satisfies
\[
 |S(C)|\ \ge\
 b+\left\lceil\frac{S_2+T/D_2}{W}\right\rceil.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The number of maximal totally isotropic subspaces of a split
$2h$-space is
\[
 L_h=2\prod_{j=1}^{h-1}(p^j+1),\qquad L_0=1.
\]
For completeness, the hyperbolic equation is $x\cdot y=0$ with
$x,y\in\mathbb F_p^h$. Its nonzero solutions, modulo scalar, number
$(p^h-1)(p^{h-1}+1)/(p-1)$: when $x\ne0$ there are $p^{h-1}$
choices of $y$, and when $x=0$ there are $p^h-1$ nonzero choices.
Each maximal isotropic subspace has $(p^h-1)/(p-1)$ projective points.
Counting incidences at one point, whose quotient has dimension
$2(h-1)$, gives $L_h=(p^{h-1}+1)L_{h-1}$.
There are two isotropic lines when $h=1$, giving the formula.

A specified isotropic point belongs to a uniformly chosen generator
with probability $L_{h-1}/L_h=1/D_1$. Two distinct orthogonal points
span an isotropic plane and co-occur with probability
$L_{h-2}/L_h=1/(D_1D_2)$. Nonorthogonal points never co-occur.
Expanding $Y=\sum_\ell w_\ell\,1_{\{\ell\subset C/G\}}$ proves both
moment formulas. Finally $Y^2\le(\max Y)Y$, so the maximum is at least
$\mathbb E Y^2/\mathbb E Y$, with the stated integer rounding.
\end{proof}

If $G$ contains five orthogonal real sign rows, the parity theorem
of the tenth pass makes the sign classes of each completion code
pairwise orthogonal over the integers. Thus the condition
\[
 b+\left\lceil(S_2+T/D_2)/W\right\rceil\ge N-3
\]
is sufficient for a Hadamard matrix, by the earlier three-row
completion theorem. It is a count certificate, not a uniform
existence theorem: a proof still has to supply a $G$ and sufficiently
large counts. Finite-polar-space incidence is classical; the
calculation is included to expose precisely the needed input.
The code-neighbor graph is a classical hyperbolic dual polar graph;
see the dual-polar section of Brouwer et al.,
\emph{The smallest eigenvalues of Hamming graphs, Johnson graphs and
other distance-regular graphs with classical parameters},
\url{https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jctb.2018.04.005}.
No priority is claimed for these incidence or spectral ingredients.

\subsection{Exact benchmark where a second moment helps}
Use the first eight stored rows of the twelve-row order-twenty
terminal frame in
\path{../round10/code_traps/search.json}. Their residue span has
dimension eight and contains $b=9$ sign classes. Here $h=2$, and
exact enumeration gives
\[
 W=42,\qquad S_2=96,\qquad T=588,\qquad D_1=6,\quad D_2=2.
\]
The mean total sign count is $9+7=16$, below $N-3=17$.
The second-moment bound instead gives
\[
 \max |S(C)|\ge9+\lceil65/7\rceil=19,
\]
and therefore certifies a full completion. This is a known-order
benchmark, not an unknown-order construction.

Retaining only the first five rows gives $h=5$, $b=5$, and
$W=S_2=1092$, $T=370512$. The same bound certifies only nine total
classes, short of seventeen. A large pool alone does not suffice;
its incidence structure and the retained space both matter.
At order twelve the checker exhausts all eight self-dual completions
of a specified rank-four retained space, verifying both moment
identities directly. Their additional sign counts are all eight.

\subsection{An exact mean drift for random neighbors}
The restricted-neighbor identity of the tenth pass implies a useful
diagnostic. Fix the retained space $G$ and choose each next code
uniformly among self-dual neighbors containing $G$. Define
\[
 d_h=\frac{p^h-1}{p-1},\qquad
 \mu=b+\frac{W}{1+p^{h-1}},\qquad
 \rho_h=\frac{d_h-1-p^{h-1}}{d_h}.
\]
\begin{proposition}[Exact expected sign count along the walk]
For every starting code $C_0\supset G$ and every integer $t\ge0$,
\[
 \mathbb E |S(C_t)|=\mu+\rho_h^t(|S(C_0)|-\mu).
\]
For $h=2$, $\rho_h=0$. For $h\ge3$, $0<\rho_h<1/p$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The preceding pass proved
\[
 \sum_{C'\sim C,\ C'\supset G}(|S(C')|-|S(C)|)
 =b+W-|S(C)|-(|S(C)|-b)p^{h-1}.
\]
There are $d_h$ such neighbors. Dividing and rearranging gives
$\mathbb E[|S(C_{t+1})|\mid C_t]
=\mu+\rho_h(|S(C_t)|-\mu)$. Iterate conditional expectations.
For $h\ge2$,
$\rho_h=(p^{h-1}-p)/(p^h-1)$, which gives the stated bounds.
\end{proof}

When a code has more than the global mean number of sign classes,
an unguided neighbor step lowers that number in expectation.
This statement does not rule out improving neighbors or guided
search; the exact order-twenty census in the other branch finds
such neighbors. The formula explains why unrestricted averaging
does not itself prove an improving move. No assertion of universal
positive drift or of all-order Hadamard existence follows.

\section{The full neighbor census of a specified terminal code}

Fix the self-dual $[20,10]$ code $C$ over $\mathbb F_5$ certified in
round10's \texttt{code\_traps/search.json}. It has twelve antipodal
sign classes, forming a terminal real orthogonal frame and spanning
$C$. Here a neighbor means a different self-dual code with
intersection dimension nine.

\begin{theorem}[Syndrome census identity]
For a self-dual code $C$ of dimension $r$ over an odd prime field,
each hyperplane $H\subset C$ determines exactly one other self-dual
code $C_H$ containing $H$. If $B$ is a basis of $C$ and $v$ is an
isotropic vector outside $C$, its nonzero projective syndrome
$[Bv^T]$ labels $H=C\cap v^\perp$ and hence $C_H$.
Consequently counting all sign vectors outside $C$ by projective
syndrome counts all new sign classes in each neighbor. Adding the
old sign classes annihilated by that syndrome gives its entire
sign count. Neighbors with no outside sign cannot improve the count.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The nondegenerate quotient $H^\perp/H$ has dimension two and contains
the isotropic line $C/H$, hence is a hyperbolic plane. Its quadratic
form has exactly two isotropic lines. The other one uniquely defines
$C_H/H$. The functional $c\mapsto c\cdot v$ has kernel $H$;
its projective coordinate in the dual basis is the syndrome.
Any isotropic $v\in H^\perp\setminus C$ belongs to the other line,
so all such vectors belong to that same unique neighbor.
\end{proof}
At length twenty every sign vector is isotropic modulo five because
its squared norm is twenty. Normalize its first coordinate to $+1$.
There are $2^{19}=524288$ such vectors. The script \texttt{census.py}
visits all of them in Gray order, updating the ten-coordinate
syndrome by one signed code column at each step. Twelve have zero
syndrome; the rest occupy 417826 projective syndrome classes.

\begin{theorem}[Complete improving-neighbor census for this code]
Of the $(5^{10}-1)/4=2441406$ self-dual neighbors of $C$, exactly
42 have more than twelve sign classes. Every one of those has
twenty sign classes and forms a real Hadamard matrix. Ten retain
ten old rows and add ten; thirty-two retain nine old rows and add
eleven. Thus the code is not a local maximum for sign count, but
every improving move loses at least two old rows.
There are 2678 tied neighbors with twelve sign classes.
The complete neighbor sign-count distribution is
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
\text{sign count}&0&1&2&3&4&5\\\hline
\text{neighbors}&94504&426152&792872&559560&330124&130000
\end{array}
\]
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrr}
\text{sign count}&6&8&12&20&\text{all other counts}\\\hline
\text{neighbors}&77096&28378&2678&42&0
\end{array}
\]
\end{theorem}

The Gray scan counts every neighbor with at least one outside sign.
To recover the others without scanning all $5^{10}$ syndromes, form
the length-twelve evaluation code
$D=\{(\alpha(c_1),\ldots,\alpha(c_{12})): \alpha\in C^*\}$,
where $c_i$ are the old sign representatives. Their spanning property
makes $D$ ten-dimensional; its dual is two-dimensional. Directly
enumerating its 25 words gives dual weight distribution
$1+8z^8+8z^{10}+8z^{12}$.
The MacWilliams identity~\cite{MW} recovers the weight counts of $D$:
\[
 A_w(D)=\frac1{25}\sum_j A_j(D^\perp)
 \sum_{\ell=0}^w(-1)^\ell4^{w-\ell}
       \binom j\ell\binom{12-j}{w-\ell}.
\]
A nonzero evaluation word with $k$ zero entries represents a
hyperplane retaining $k$ old signs, and division by four removes
scalar multiples. Subtracting the Gray-scan counts for each retention
number gives exactly the neighbors with no outside signs. Every
integer and divisibility assertion is checked. All 42 improving
codes are additionally rebuilt by explicit neighbor pivots, and
their complete sign lists and real Hadamard Gram identities checked.

The runtime is a bounded finite enumeration, not an asymptotic search
or a theorem about arbitrary code landscapes. In particular it does
not rule out suboptimal local maxima at other codes or orders.
It supplies a complete local landscape at one explicit terminal
frame, with efficient general syndrome bookkeeping and no unknown
Hadamard-existence assumption.
\subsection{The exact row-retention budget from generalized Hamming weights}
For the same twelve sign representatives $c_1,\ldots,c_{12}$ spanning
$C$, let
\[
 E=\{(\alpha(c_1),\ldots,\alpha(c_{12})): \alpha\in C^*\}
 \subseteq\mathbb F_5^{12}.
\]
This is the ten-dimensional evaluation code used in the neighbor
census. A codimension-$h$ retained subspace $G\subset C$ has
annihilator $U\subset C^*$ of dimension $h$. Its lost old rows are
exactly the union of the supports of the evaluation words of $U$:
\[
 c_i\notin G\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists\alpha\in U:\alpha(c_i)\ne0.
\]
Consequently the minimum possible number of lost old sign classes
among all such $G$ is precisely the classical generalized Hamming
weight $d_h(E)$, defined as the least support size of an
$h$-dimensional subcode. This is a standard coding-theoretic resource
interpretation, not a claim to a new generalized-weight theory.
The classical reference is V. K. Wei, \emph{Generalized Hamming weights
for linear codes}, IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 37 (1991),
1412--1418, \url{https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/133259/}.

The two-dimensional dual has weight enumerator
$1+8z^8+8z^{10}+8z^{12}$. Its parity-check matrix $H$ has no zero
columns and projective column multiplicities $4,4,2,2,0,0$ among
the six projective points of $\mathbb F_5^2$. The final two zeros
denote absent projective directions, not zero columns. For a set
$I$ of column positions,
\[
 \dim\{e\in E:\operatorname{supp}(e)\subseteq I\}
 =|I|-\operatorname{rank}H_I,
\]
so $d_h(E)=\min\{|I|:|I|-\operatorname{rank}H_I\ge h\}$.
Any nonempty $I$ has rank at least one; rank-one sets have at most
four columns. They yield $d_1,d_2,d_3=2,3,4$. For $h\ge4$ the
rank must be two and $d_h=h+2$, attainable by choosing that many
columns. Thus
\[
 (d_1,\ldots,d_{10})=(2,3,4,6,7,8,9,10,11,12).
\]
The exact maximum retained-row counts for codimensions one through
ten are therefore $10,9,8,6,5,4,3,2,1,0$.
In particular the rank-eight, nine-sign-class benchmark used in the
completion-moment calculation has the largest possible old-row
retention for a two-dimensional annihilator. A retained space
containing five old rows can have codimension at most five.

The standard-library script \texttt{check\_retention.py} independently
checks all 4096 column subsets, writes the full witnesses to
\texttt{retention.json}, and verifies both the weight enumerator and
projective multiplicities. These are optimal retention budgets for
this specific code. They do not ensure that any corresponding
completion improves sign count or prove unknown-order existence.

\section{What is still missing}
The sharp global repair family rules out an automatic small-change
conversion of arbitrary isotropic laws to Hadamard mixtures; it
does not restrict all possible global constructions.
The quotient grid solves compatibility once a suitable retained
space and modular pool have been supplied. The moment certificate
still needs favorable counts, and the neighbor census covers one
code at one known order.

A proof of the full conjecture still requires an all-order
construction, or a uniform count or exchange theorem that reaches
the extraction threshold without assuming a desired matrix already
exists. The new tools do not supply that theorem. All finite
checks use exact arithmetic, not Lean; no external proof code,
Git commits, submissions or publications were made.
