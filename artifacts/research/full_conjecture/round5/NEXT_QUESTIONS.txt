RESEARCH TARGETS AFTER THE RESIDUE-SENSITIVE ENTROPY THEOREM

1. Priority and interpretation.
Compare the precise collision bound and equality classification with
Lancaster1965, Gavinsky--Pudlak2016, Delsarte bounds, weighted binary
orthogonal arrays, and Bierbrauer--Gopalakrishnan--Stinson1996.
The weighted-frame and parity mechanisms are classical tools; an
independent derivation does not establish novelty.

2. Attainment, rather than another necessary bound.
Prove an exact or sufficiently accurate upper bound on minimum joint
entropy for d=4m-1 unbiased pairwise-independent bits. At present the
proved direction is the lower bound. A measure in the explicit
rounding window would already imply H(4m). Pairwise independence,
optimal min-entropy of a single atom, and convex averaging do not
supply that measure.

3. Stability of the residue equality classification.
At n=1 mod4 equality makes parity a linear combination of augmented
coordinates and leaves only tetrahedral residual types. Quantify what
near equality forces without assuming that low-weight bad atoms can
be removed while preserving exact moments. A useful theorem would
combine arithmetic rounding with control of the loss of isotropy.

4. Residual Gram forms and carefully selected partial matrices.
Completion up to seven missing rows is classical and eight can fail.
Classify which extra hypotheses on residual rank-eight Gram forms
avoid the known unextendible examples. Rational and integral
completions alone do not force sign completions. No E8 equivalence
has been established by this pass.

5. Sign-sensitive capacities or moment formulations.
Ordinary capacities of squared orthogonal entries equal1 universally.
The exact quantum hierarchy must retain a two-copy coincidence
defect; the one-copy maximum-entropy state can average away all
flatness. Any useful existence principle must preserve the missing
factor-level sign information and prove feasibility, rather than
assuming it in a reformulation.
